# Brand Constitution — French for Punjabi Immigrants (working name)

## 1. Audience definition

**Primary (≈95%):** Punjabi international students in Canada, mostly women, who finished their studies and are now on a work permit. Two dominant archetypes:
- New mothers / pregnant women who cannot work full-time and see French as their PR pathway.
- Fresh grads (12th grade done in Punjab) with no other route to PR.

**The trigger moment:** 4–8 months left on the work permit. They avoided French until the deadline forced them. They arrive scared, skeptical, and time-poor.

**What they're really buying:** Not a language. Time in Canada. CLB 5 = 2-year work permit extension (~4 months of study from zero). CLB 7 = PR pathway (6+ months).

**The mindset to respect:** Deadline-driven, trust-driven (familiar faces over big institutions), referral-driven, price-sensitive but outcome-desperate — they will pay more for the school they believe actually delivers CLB 7. Long-runway students (2+ years on permit) are churn risks: the pull to work and send money home beats studying. Content and sales analysis must treat "months left on permit" as the single most important segmentation variable.

**Explicitly NOT the audience:** hobby learners, Alliance Française shoppers, people comparing apps like Duolingo.

## 2. Positioning (the one sentence)

A real French speaker you can trust with your future — an Amritdhari Sikh who teaches in Punjabi and proves his fluency on camera, in a market full of exam-crackers who sell cheap courses in a language they can't actually speak.

Three trust pillars: (1) visible authenticity (the face, the identity), (2) demonstrated fluency (French spoken in every video — this is the attack competitors can't copy), (3) student referrals and results.

## 3. Banned topics (checked before any suggestion or draft)

1. **Guaranteed immigration outcomes.** Never promise PR, permit extension, or visa approval. Conditional score guarantees are allowed only in the established voice pattern: "If you give 5–6 hours daily and stay consistent, I guarantee you reach CLB 5–6" — effort condition stated, score not visa.
2. **Immigration legal advice stated as fact.** Pathways may be described generally ("CLB 5 can help with extension"); eligibility rulings belong to an RCIC/lawyer.
3. **Naming and attacking specific competitor schools.** Attack the pattern ("exam-crackers who don't speak French"), never a name.
4. **Fabricated urgency or fake discounts.** Real urgency (permit expiry math) is encouraged — it's true. Invented countdowns and fake "last seat" claims are banned.
5. **Political and religious controversy.** Punjab/India politics, Khalistan, Canadian immigration politics. The teacher's Sikh identity is presented with pride; it is never used as a political statement.

## 4. Tone rules (concrete, testable)

1. **Blunt on facts, warm on fear.** Price, hours, structure, timelines → direct, no padding. Doubt and fear ("will I manage with a baby?") → connect with the emotion first, then give the plan.
2. **Mirror the student's language.** Punjabi if they speak Punjabi, English if English. Default video language: Punjabi, with spoken French in every video as fluency proof.
3. **Every guarantee carries its condition.** Never "you will pass." Always "IF you give X hours and stay consistent, THEN I guarantee Y score." The condition is said out loud, not buried.
4. **Always end with one concrete next step.** Free demo, structure walkthrough, or "message me your permit expiry date." Never end on motivation alone.
5. **Use the deadline math, honestly.** "You have 5 months. CLB 5 needs 4. Here is the day-by-day." Real numbers create real urgency; no invented scarcity.
6. **Prove, don't claim.** Speak French on camera, show student results, show the portal. "Best course" said = weak; fluency shown = strong.
7. **Respect the grind, never mock it.** Students juggling babies, night shifts, and money sent home are the heroes of every story. No shame, no "you're lazy," no fear-mongering about deportation.
8. **Structure is the sales pitch.** When explaining the course, always walk the ladder: grammar month → 15-day intermediate (think in French, 2–3 min conversations) → advanced (2.5 hr/day, portal level-analysis, live native-speaker review). Transparency about the path is the differentiator from exam-crackers.

## 5. Five example posts that nail the voice

1. **(Reel, Punjabi + French)** "Tuhade kol 5 mahine bache ne? Watch me order a full meal in French — *Bonjour, je voudrais un café et un croissant, s'il vous plaît* — this level is CLB 5, and 4 months is enough IF you give 5–6 hours daily. Free demo, link in bio."
   *Why it works: deadline math + fluency proof + conditional guarantee + one next step (rules 3, 4, 5, 6).*
2. **(Reel, student story)** "She came with 6 months left and a 8-month-old baby. She studied while the baby slept. Last week: CLB 6. Her words, not mine — [clip of student]. If she found the hours, tell me your situation."
   *Why it works: hero is the grinding student, proof over claim, warm not preachy (rules 6, 7).*
3. **(Talking head, blunt)** "Sach sunna chaunde ho? If your permit ends in 3 months, CLB 7 is not realistic. CLB 5 is. I will not take your money for a dream I can't deliver. Message me your expiry date, I'll tell you honestly what's possible."
   *Why it works: blunt on facts, honest refusal builds trust, concrete next step (rules 1, 4, 8 spirit).*
4. **(Reel, positioning attack — no names)** "Bahut saare teachers ne bas TEF crack kita, French nahi sikhi. Ask any teacher one thing before paying: 'Speak French with me for 2 minutes, right now.' Here's my 2 minutes — [speaks French]. Now go ask them."
   *Why it works: attacks the pattern not a name, converts differentiator into a test the viewer can run (rules 6, banned #3 respected).*
5. **(Carousel/long post, structure)** "Exactly what your 4 months look like: Month 1 — grammar, full foundation. Next 15 days — you start SPEAKING, 2–3 minute conversations, brain trained to think in French. Then advanced: 2.5 hrs/day, portal shows exactly how far you are from your target band, live review with a native speaker. No mystery, no 'trust me.' Demo is free."
   *Why it works: structure-as-pitch, transparency vs exam-crackers, ends with next step (rules 4, 8).*

## 6. Five example posts that VIOLATE the voice

1. "Join us and your PR is 100% GUARANTEED! CLB 7 pakka!"
   *Violation: guarantees an immigration outcome with no effort condition — banned topic #1, legal exposure.*
2. "XYZ French Academy teachers are frauds, don't go there."
   *Violation: names and attacks a competitor — banned #3; attack the pattern, never the name.*
3. "⚠️ ONLY 2 SEATS LEFT! Price doubles at midnight!" (when neither is true)
   *Violation: fabricated urgency — banned #4; real deadline math is the honest urgency we own.*
4. "If you don't learn French NOW you will be deported back to Punjab with nothing."
   *Violation: fear-mongering and mocking the audience's situation — breaks rule 7; we use deadlines, not humiliation.*
5. "Unlock your best self with the transformative power of language learning! ✨"
   *Violation: generic Canadian-brand voice — no Punjabi, no proof, no deadline math, no next step; sounds like every other school (breaks rules 2, 4, 6).*
